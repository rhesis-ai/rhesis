# Reports

A report summarises one endpoint's test runs against the requirements you pick: the pass rate overall and per requirement, what wasn't tested, and what a reader should know before trusting the numbers. Teams use it to decide whether a release can ship, or to write up a set of experiments.

## Draft and published

A report starts as a **draft**. Its numbers are live: they update as runs finish and as reviewers change verdicts. You can still change its runs, requirements, counting rule and text.

**Publishing** freezes the report:

- the counted results, the numbers and the endpoint's configuration (without secrets) are stored with the report and no longer change;
- a SHA-256 snapshot hash is computed over everything the report shows; it appears on the report page and in the footer of every PDF page, so a PDF can be matched to the report it came from;
- the report can no longer be edited or deleted.

To prepare the next release, open a published report and choose **Copy as new draft**. The copy keeps the endpoint, runs or run tag, requirements and text, and is compared with the report it was copied from.

## Release and general reports

A **release report** ends with a recommendation: *Ready to ship*, *Ready to ship, with known risks* or *Not ready to ship*. A **general report**, for example a write-up of experiments, has no recommendation. You choose when you create the report and can change it while it's a draft.

## Export as PDF

**Export PDF** downloads the report as a PDF: a summary page with the recommendation, key numbers and requirements, followed by the details (runs and metrics). A draft's PDF is marked *DRAFT* on every page.

## Permissions

Viewing a report needs `report:read`. Members and higher roles can create, edit, publish and export reports; Viewers and Auditors can only read them. See [Roles](/docs/organizations/roles).

## Enterprise Edition

<Callout type="info">
  These parts of a report are Enterprise Edition features.
</Callout>

- **By impact**: pass rate per impact, and an Impact column in the requirements table. An impact is the kind of harm a failing [requirement](/docs/requirements) causes, such as a reputation, compliance or privacy impact. A requirement without its own impact counts under its parent's. (The screenshots use severity names as impacts; in your organization they are your impact categories.)
- **Who was involved**: the author, who ran the tests and who reviewed results (release reports only).
- **Stats**: results judged and errored, and response times.
- **Audit trail**: publishing and every PDF export are recorded in the [audit log](/docs/organizations/audit-log).
- **Your branding**: the PDF carries your organization's product name, colour and logo instead of Rhesis.

A published report keeps these sections in its PDF even if the organization's plan changes later.
